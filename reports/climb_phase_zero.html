Environment report · grounded adaptive motion curricula

CLIMB Phase Zero

The training stack is installed and measured, the motion bank is being rebuilt, and three silent data-corruption bugs were caught before any of them could invalidate the experiment matrix.

2026-08-15 RTX 5090 · sm_120 · 32 GiB driver 590.48.01 / CUDA 13.1 20 cores · 93 GB

01 What changed

Four findings that alter the plan as written.

Caught & fixed

Every existing motion file was silently wrong

All 7,901 pre-existing G1 .npz files use Isaac Lab breadth-first body order; mjlab needs MuJoCo depth-first. Same shape, so it loads, trains, and reports plausible rewards — against the wrong links.

Caught & fixed

The AMASS retargets are unusable as shipped

That directory mixes six frame rates encoded per filename — one --input-fps retimes 59% of the bank — and stores root height relative, burying the robot 0.75 m into the floor.

Fixed

mjlab could not be installed at all

Not a stale venv — its lockfile pinned a MuJoCo nightly that has been deleted from the index (HTTP 404). Resolved by moving to v1.6.0, which also aligns mujoco-warp with Newton at 3.11.0.

Measured

Compute is 6–13× cheaper than budgeted

0.45 s/iteration at 4,096 envs → 3.8 GPU-hours per run, against the plan's assumed 24–48. The full matrix is ~169 GPU-hours, not 1,080–2,160.

Needs a decision

H2 contradicts the prior evidence

Three earlier results on this machine tested essentially this mechanism and found null-to-adverse. The plan cites none of them. See §07.

Three seeds cannot produce a significant result: the minimum achievable one-sided sign-flip p at n=3 is 0.125, and the standing guardrail requires ≥5. The compute objection to running 5 seeds does not survive measurement — the 5-seed design fits with headroom even at 50% duty.

Caveats. Single-clip measurement. A multi-clip bank adds motion tensors to VRAM (~12 MiB per two-minute clip; 800 short AMASS clips ≈ 0.8 GiB, still comfortable) and may change reset behaviour. Whether 30k iterations is the right convergence budget for a multi-clip policy is untested — calibrate before freezing the matrix.

04 Three silent corruptions

mjlab's MotionLoader indexes body_pos_w with indices from the compiled MuJoCo model and validates nothing. There is no body_names key in the schema and no assertion anywhere in the tracking task.

0 123 456 789 1011 1213 1415 1617 1819 2021 2223 2425 2627 2829
left side right side pelvis / spine ankle-roll (feet)

Both layouts are exactly 30 bodies × 29 joints, so shapes match, the file loads cleanly, training runs, and reward curves look plausible — while every tracking target is bound to the wrong link.

The discriminator

The validator classifies on the pelvis-frame lateral offset of three proximal bodies. Their origins sit at fixed or hinge-invariant transforms from the pelvis, so their pelvis-frame y does not move whatever the robot is doing. Measured across all 40 LAFAN1 clips — falls, fights and jumps included — the spread is exactly zero:

Ordery[1]y[2]y[7]
depth-first+0.0645+0.1165−0.0645
breadth-first+0.0645−0.0645+0.1238

sign(y[2]) alone separates them. Two weaker heuristics were tried and rejected: "the two lowest bodies are the feet" misfires on kneeling and sitting motions; whole-limb mean y plus correlation against the neutral pose wrongly rejected 7 of the first 549 AMASS conversions — all legitimate calibration T-poses, Lie_Down, and martial-arts kicks, i.e. exactly the clips whose legs cross the midline.

The lesson generalises: a motion-bank validator has to be invariant to pose, because the hard clips — the ones a curriculum most wants — are precisely the ones that break pose-dependent heuristics.

Clips and hours are not interchangeable. AMASS clips have a median of 7.3 s against LAFAN1's 238 s, so an 800-clip AMASS bank is roughly 1.6 h of motion while 77 LAFAN1 clips are 4.6 h. The experiment matrix specifies bank size in clips — worth pinning down which quantity the scaling axis is actually meant to be before it is frozen.

Do not merge the two LAFAN1 sets. bank/lafan1 (40 clips) and bank/lafan1_gmr (77 clips) are different retargetings of the same source motions and overlap on 40 names. Mixing them puts two retargeting pipelines inside a single experimental condition.

The GMR set is worth the trouble: it validated 77/77 at 4.60 hours — the full official LAFAN1 duration the plan assumes, versus 2.45 h from the other retarget — and it unlocks 14 families absent from the 40-clip set: ground1/2, obstacles1–6, push1, pushAndFall1, pushAndStumble1, aiming1/2, multipleActions1. Precisely the low-posture, multi-contact and recovery motions the plan wants for its hard set and its gentler hardware demos.

A second silent corruption: the AMASS retargets do not share a frame rate

mjlab's converter takes a single --input-fps, and the obvious reading of train_converted_complete/ is that it is 120 fps throughout. It is not — the rate is encoded per file in the name, and the directory mixes six of them:

SuffixClipsEffect if read as 120 fps
_120_4,403correct
_100_4,16520% too fast
_60_6422× too fast
_250_56~2× too slow
_59_ , _150_22wrong
GRAB (no suffix)~1,400unknown; treated as 120

Converting the directory at one rate silently retimes 59% of the bank. Nothing errors — the clip just plays at the wrong speed, and because velocity and acceleration are finite-differenced during conversion, every kinematic and dynamic feature, and every tracking target the policy sees, is wrong by that factor. It is the timing analogue of the body-order bug: the shapes stay valid, so nothing catches it.

--infer-fps now reads the rate per clip from the filename and records it in the manifest, falling back to --input-fps (and reporting how often) for names that carry none. Verified on a 250 fps clip: 7,471 input frames now yield 29.9 s of output instead of the 62 s a 120 fps reading produced. The first build was stopped and audited rather than trusted — of 4,261 clips already written, 3,597 came from genuine 120 fps sources and were kept; the 664 mis-timed ones were deleted and requeued.

A third: the AMASS retargets store root height relative

Root z in train_converted_complete/*.csv averages −0.004 m. In the LAFAN1 CSVs, which use the identical column layout, it averages +0.767 m. The AMASS files record height relative to the standing pose; the LAFAN1 files record it absolutely. Converted as-is the G1 is buried to the pelvis — on a "Stand" clip the shins sit 0.51 m below the floor plane and the deepest geom 0.79 m below it. The body-order validator passes these files, correctly: the ordering is fine, only the height is wrong.

The fix needs no re-conversion. A root-height change is a rigid vertical translation, so it applies to a finished npz — every body moves by the same amount and joint angles, orientations and all velocities are untouched. ground_align_bank.py shifts each clip by the 1st percentile of its lower foot's clearance, so a clip with genuine flight phases aligns on its touchdowns rather than its apex.

BankMedian offsetReading
LAFAN1−0.012 malready grounded
AMASS−0.752 mburied by the missing standing pelvis height

That AMASS median is the standing pelvis height almost exactly — the signature of this specific bug rather than of scattered retargeting noise. LAFAN1 is deliberately left unaligned: its 12 mm penetration is present in mjlab's own published reference export too, and that bank is currently bit-equivalent to it. Trading a verified match against upstream for 12 mm is a bad deal; the AMASS bank has no such reference and needs the shift.

Two measurement bugs found while building this

Both were caught by checking family ordering against physics rather than assuming it:

  • Joint-torque utilisation was invalid and has been removed. Retargeted mocap does not place the feet consistently on the terrain — most frames report ncon = 0 — so MuJoCo's constraint solve contributes nothing and inverse dynamics dumps the full 327 N bodyweight into the root residual. One standing frame produced 354 N·m at a 139 N·m motor. Replaced with quantities that are well defined without a consistent contact state: required friction coefficient, vertical GRF in bodyweights, and CoP margin, computed with contacts explicitly disabled so the free-joint block of qfrc_inverse is the required ground wrench.
  • Contact is now measured, not inferred. Three successive height heuristics each failed on a different family: "the two lowest bodies are the feet" misfires on kneeling and sitting; a min foot-height ground reference is destroyed by one penetrating frame (GMR dips to −6 mm on fight1, making 95% of it read as flight); a low percentile over all bodies is meaningless while lying down and pushed walk2 to 0.36 flight. The terrain is a plane at exactly z = 0 and an exact mj_geomDistance query costs ~0.1 s for a 13k-frame clip, so every frame is now queried against real collision geometry. No ground estimation remains.

The two retargets are not interchangeable — and the atlas proves it

With exact contact, cross-retarget rank agreement over the 15 shared families splits sharply:

FeatureSpearman ρ
non-foot ground contact+0.964
required friction μ+0.918

The flight disagreement is a property of the data, not the tool. Median lower-foot clearance is +3.3 mm for the whole_body_tracking retarget and +20.9 mm for GMR — GMR floats the robot about 2 cm off the floor, so its feet rarely enter the 2 cm contact band and every contact-derived feature is inflated. walk4 reads 91% airborne under GMR and 0% under wbt.

This is now exposed as the foot_clearance_p1 / p50 / penetration_max covariates — which is exactly the "retargeting residual magnitude" feature §3.3 asks for. GMR clips need a per-clip z-offset correction before use as tracking references; a policy trained on them would learn to hover. The correction is deliberately not applied automatically, since it alters the reference the policy tracks.

Caveat: the support polygon is approximated by the two ankle-roll positions, ignoring the ~0.2 × 0.1 m foot area, so CoP margins run pessimistic by about half a foot length.

20% of the bank is physically untrackable

Geometric validity is not physical validity. These clips have correct body order, correct timing and correct grounding, and still demand things no controller can deliver — kinematic retargeting carries no dynamics constraint, so it will happily produce a motion needing friction coefficient 13 or 43 bodyweights of ground reaction. Left in, they occupy the hard tail of the difficulty atlas for entirely the wrong reason.

06 First training result

Everything above is instrumentation. This is the first actual experiment, and it changes what the plan should do next.

The multi-clip capability now exists

climb/ is an mjlab extension — the §7 deliverable — adding the axis CLIMB needs. MotionBank concatenates clips and keeps a per-clip offset table, so time_steps becomes a global index and all ~20 of mjlab's accessors work unchanged; only the clip axis is new, since mjlab already samples the start frame within a clip.

TaskSamplerVerified
Climb-…-G1uniform clip, uniform frametop-1 = 0.0200 = 1/50, entropy 1.000
Climb-…-G1-Adaptiveclip ∝ failure EMA + ε/Nentropy 1.000→0.933, top-1 0.020→0.112

Uniform means uniform over clips, not frames: the bank spans 3.7 s to 264 s, so frame-uniform would weight by duration and quietly make the control arm a length-weighted curriculum. Baseline run — 4000 iterations, 4096 envs, 50 clips, ~57 min — took mean reward 1.2 → 22.4 and episode length 60 → 409 steps.

The headroom gate decides whether the matrix is interpretable

One frozen policy, evaluated across three banks, 16 episodes per clip:

BankMean survivalFrontier (≥0.20) Mastered (≥0.30)Verdict
tier_500.9150.0600.880FAIL nothing left to learn
tier_hard500.6030.4800.380PASS
tier_mixed1000.7460.2300.640PASS

Two banks clear the SIM-D1 gate that four prior candidates failed. The gate is not a formality: on tier_50 the policy masters 88% of clips after 4000 iterations — 13% of a full run — so an adaptive sampler there has nothing to prioritise between, and any sampler comparison run on it returns an uninterpretable null. That is the same shape as the prior program's SIM-M3 result.

Bank construction, not sampler design, is what buys headroom. Stratifying evenly across the difficulty composite produced a bank that is too easy; the hardest decile produced a real frontier. That is a concrete prescription the plan currently lacks.

H1: partially supported — and the half that fails is the interesting half

Measured difficulty regressed on the offline atlas over tier_mixed100 (n = 100, 5-fold cross-validated, ridge = 1.0):

Feature setkCV R²CV ρ
clip length alone1−0.039−0.144
kinematic magnitude5+0.263+0.744

H1's two clauses come apart. "Better than clip length alone" is strongly supported — length is worse than predicting the mean (ρ = −0.14) while the atlas reaches ρ ≈ 0.74 out of fold, so the featurisation earns its place. "Dynamic beats kinematic magnitude" is not supported here: kinematic is marginally ahead, and combining the two adds essentially nothing (+0.265 vs +0.263), which says they are largely redundant on this bank rather than complementary.

One seed, one policy, one bank, 4000 of 30,000 iterations, n = 100. A direction, not a result — but the direction the paper should be pressure-testing, and now cheap to test properly.

Three measurement errors caught along the way

Each produced confident, plausible, wrong numbers:

  1. Scrambled per-clip assignment. arange(n) % n_clips interleaves clips across envs, but reshape(n_clips, k) groups consecutive envs, so each "clip" row averaged k different clips. Aggregate means stayed correct, which is why it survived a first look. Caught because the hard and easy halves of a mixed bank came out with identical mean difficulty (0.364 vs 0.371) after measuring 0.380 vs 0.886 when evaluated separately.
  2. Difficulty measured at the wrong unit. Starting every episode at frame 0 makes a clip's 16 episodes near-identical, so survival collapses to 0 or 1 and the frontier band empties. Sampling start points within the clip — matching how training and any curriculum sample — moved tier_hard50 from frontier 0.100 (fail) to 0.480 (pass).
  3. A 6%-support predictor detonating the regression. nonfoot_ground_frac is nonzero in 6 of 100 clips; folds whose training split held few of them gave held-out clips enormous z-scores, taking the dynamic set from R² +0.21 to −142 while its own rank correlation was −0.04. Now excluded by a minimum-support rule applied before any set is scored.

07 Where the plan and the evidence disagree

This is the one item that needs a decision rather than a command.

H2 claims a ≥2–3× compute reduction from grounded adaptive sampling. The prior SONIC track-B program on this machine tested essentially that mechanism and recorded three non-supporting results:

ResultFinding
SIM-M3adaptive vs uniform, mean Δ MPJPE-G +0.111 (worse), 1/3 seeds improved, permutation p = 0.875 — preregistered effect gate FAIL
SIM-D1four candidate banks built, all four failed or were invalidated; frontier fractions 0/2, 0/24, 6/41, and a 9/28 pass voided on a root-XY drift guard
ZPD pilotthe literal E[p(1−p)] frontier utility CLIMB §3.4 proposes; mechanism demonstrably activated (608 vs 283 effective bins) and still lost all four normalized-AUC metrics

The baseline is stronger than "uniform"

mjlab already ships BeyondMimic-style adaptive sampling upstream, over ~1 s bins within a clip: a failure-count EMA (adaptive_alpha), non-causal kernel smoothing (adaptive_lambda), and adaptive_uniform_ratio = 0.1 — an ε-uniform floor that is already a crude grounding term. Collapse diagnostics (sampling_entropy, sampling_top1_prob, sampling_top1_bin) are already logged.

So CLIMB's real delta is narrower — and better posed

The segment axis exists. What does not exist anywhere is the clip axis (MotionLoader loads exactly one file), the horizon axis, and the grounding term as anything richer than ε-uniform.

That argues for making grounding vs ε-uniform at matched frontier the primary contrast rather than grounded-adaptive vs uniform. It is the only genuinely new part, and the frontier term it would be bolted onto has already tested null-to-adverse twice on this researcher's own data.

It also argues for adding a dataset-headroom gate to Phase 0 (frontier fraction ≥ 0.20 against a fixed reference policy). Frontier fraction — not spread — was the binding constraint in three of four prior failures, and measuring it costs a fraction of one matrix cell while determining whether the other 44 are interpretable.

09 Open decisions

  1. Re-specify the matrix at 5 seeds? The compute objection does not survive measurement; three seeds cannot reach significance.
  2. Make the grounding term the primary contrast rather than an ablation.
  3. Add the SIM-D1 headroom gate to Phase 0 before committing to the matrix.
  4. Is a physical G1 actually available, and when? Two Phase-0 exit criteria and all of Phase 3 depend on it; only an SDK checkout and a LAN address are visible from here.
  5. Disk. / is at 91%; ~95 GB sits in the uv and pip caches. Not pruned — the uv cache holds the only local copy of the now-404 MuJoCo wheel, so pruning forecloses ever rebuilding the old mjlab lock.
  6. A stray user-site torch (2.5.1+cu124) shadows every python3.10 environment and breaks the gmr conda env: it reports is_available() = True, then fails on sm_120 kernels. Left in place — renaming it affects other projects.
