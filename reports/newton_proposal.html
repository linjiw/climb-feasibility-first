01 The story so far, told straight

You started with a new physics library and a goal: use it to make humanoid research better — more stable policies, better sim2real, deeper understanding of RL training and of the physics of motion. Here is what actually happened.

Phase 0
Environment. Newton installed and verified (6142 tests, sm_120). mjlab found unfixable at its commit — lock pinned a purged MuJoCo nightly — rebuilt at v1.6.0. Both stacks aligned on mujoco-warp 3.11.0.
Phase 0
Data. 10,822 clips / 43.6 h of validated G1 motion. Three silent corruptions caught: breadth-first body order in every pre-existing file, six mixed frame rates read as one, root height stored relative. Each trained plausible reward curves against wrong data.
Phase 0
Atlas. Physics-feature featurisation of 10,705 clips. Predicts per-clip difficulty out-of-fold at ρ ≈ 0.74; clip length does not (ρ = −0.14). Multi-contact axis orthogonal to kinematic intensity (ρ = −0.10).
Exp-1
Multi-clip capability built as an mjlab extension. Uniform vs error-adaptive: adaptive loses on 3/3 seeds because it collapses 87–89% of mass onto one clip; the "10% uniform floor" is arithmetically not a floor. Filed upstream against mjlab (#1153) and BeyondMimic (#73).
Exp-2
Grounded arm. Fixes the collapse (≫ adaptive), matches uniform on AULC, edges it on endpoint. Branch B. Advisor's v2 plan queues E3/E4/E10 on samplers.
throughout
Newton's role: substrate only. It ran the G1 example and stood in as an inverse-dynamics engine — via raw MuJoCo, not even Newton's own ArticulationView. Every research question was asked and answered inside mjlab. Nothing that was learned required Newton.

The drift is real and worth naming. The curriculum line produced genuine findings — an upstream bug in two libraries, a measured collapse mechanism, a reinterpretation of your prior SIM-M3 null. But it is a sampling paper that could have been done in mjlab alone. Newton was installed, verified, and then not used for anything it is uniquely good at.

What is not lost: the bank, the hygiene tooling, the atlas methodology, the realized-exposure ledger discipline, and the pre-registration practice all carry over intact. And two of the curriculum findings turn out to be the strongest arguments for the redirect — see §03.

03 The two findings that point the way

Buried in the curriculum results are two facts about physics, not sampling.

Difficulty is a motion property that reference-motion features do not capture

Two independently trained policies rank clips by difficulty at ρ = 0.832. So "how hard is this motion for a G1" is largely intrinsic to the motion. But the atlas — kinematic magnitude plus dynamic feasibility computed from the reference alone — transfers at only ρ = 0.567. It captures two-thirds of the available signal and misses the rest.

The clearest miss: the clip every adaptive sampler collapsed onto, in all six runs (clip #44), has measured survival 0.31 against a bank mean of 0.89 — and its atlas profile is benign: μ = 0.18, peak GRF = 1.42, both low. Whatever makes it hard is not in the reference kinematics or in reference-frame inverse dynamics.

Hypothesis: what the atlas misses is the interaction — motion × robot × contact physics — which cannot be read off the reference and can only be measured by simulating it. That is exactly what Newton makes cheap: run the reference through several physics models and see where they disagree.

The frontier is temporal, and forgetting is measurable

69% of clips cross the frontier band but each dwells only ~16% of the run in it. Downward crossings — clips relearned and re-lost — order with coverage: uniform 5.6, grounded 7.7, adaptive 10.3 per 1k iterations. These are properties of the learning dynamics, and the ledger tooling that measured them transfers directly to asking the same questions about physics parameters instead of clips.

04 Proposal

Thesis. A humanoid tracking policy's stability and its sim2real transfer are governed by where in a motion the physics is fragile — segments whose outcome changes under small changes to contact, actuation, or the integrator. Newton's multi-solver, batched, differentiable simulation lets that fragility be measured per frame and per body, before deployment, and then used three ways: to diagnose why controllers like SONIC fail on specific motions, to train against physics ensembles rather than parameter jitter, and to build a physics-grounded difficulty atlas that actually transfers.

Thrust 1 — Physics fragility maps: multi-solver disagreement as a diagnostic

Run each reference motion, under a frozen tracker, through Newton's solver set — MJWarp, MuJoCo-CPU, Featherstone, XPBD — and record per-frame, per-body divergence. Where solvers agree the physics is well-conditioned; where they diverge, the outcome depends on modelling choices no simulator gets exactly right, and that is where real-robot failure should concentrate.

Hypothesis
H-F1: per-frame solver disagreement predicts real-world tracking failure better than any reference-only feature. H-F2: adding disagreement features to the atlas lifts cross-policy transfer from 0.567 toward the 0.832 ceiling.
Newton
Same Model, swap SolverMuJoCo / SolverFeatherstone / SolverXPBD. Frozen ONNX policy via warp_nn. Contact-model variation (default vs hydroelastic) as a second axis.
Feasibility gate
Does clip #44 show elevated disagreement where the benign atlas did not? One clip, four solvers, an afternoon. If not, H-F1 is in trouble early and cheaply.
Assets reused
The 10,822-clip bank; the Exp-1/2 checkpoints as frozen trackers; the SONIC release ONNX for a controller nobody here trained.

Thrust 2 — Solver-space robustness: train against physics ensembles

Standard domain randomisation jitters parameters inside one physics model. That cannot cover error in the model itself — a different contact solver, a different integrator, a different actuator abstraction. Newton makes the engine a randomisable variable. The question is whether policies trained against a solver ensemble transfer better than parameter-DR policies, and whether the gain lands on exactly the fragile segments Thrust 1 identifies.

Hypothesis
H-S1: sim2sim degradation across held-out solvers is a better predictor of sim2real degradation than parameter-DR width. H-S2: solver-ensemble training reduces held-out-solver degradation at matched compute.
Newton
Evaluation-side first: every mjlab checkpoint evaluated across all solvers (cheap, no training). Training-side is harder — mjlab trains in MJWarp only — so ensemble training would proceed by solver-mixed evaluation-driven curriculum or by a Newton-side rollout worker. This is the thrust with the most engineering risk and it should be sequenced last.
Feasibility gate
Do the Exp-1/2 checkpoints degrade differently across solvers at all? If uniform/grounded/adaptive all lose the same fraction under Featherstone, solver-space carries no signal about the policy and H-S1 dies cheaply.
Honest caveat
PhysX is not available. Solver-space robustness is a proxy for sim2real, not sim2real. The real-G1 loop through SONIC's deploy path is the only ground truth, and it needs hardware access I cannot verify from here.

Thrust 3 — Differentiable sensitivity: what does the policy actually depend on?

If Newton's backward pass works at G1 scale, tracking error becomes differentiable with respect to friction, link mass, contact stiffness, and PD gains. That gives a per-motion sensitivity vector — a physics-grounded description of why a clip is hard — and, over training, a picture of which physical dependencies the policy acquires and when.

Hypothesis
H-D1: clips with high sensitivity to contact stiffness are the ones that fail sim2real; those sensitive only to gains do not. H-D2: sensitivity structure explains the atlas's transfer gap (0.567 → 0.832).
Newton
wp.Tape through SolverMuJoCo or SolverFeatherstone; differentiable_contacts.py. Requires enable_backward=True, which mjlab turns off.
Feasibility gate
Hard gate, spike first. Does d(tracking error)/d(friction) come back finite and stable for a 10 s G1 clip with 68 contact geoms? Unknown. If no, Thrust 3 degrades to finite-difference sensitivity — still useful, far more expensive — and the proposal says so up front rather than at month three.

How the thrusts fit together

Thrust 1 produces the fragility map. Thrust 3 explains it in terms of physical parameters. Thrust 2 uses it to make policies robust where it matters. The atlas becomes a physics-grounded object with a measured transfer ceiling to aim at, and the curriculum work reappears as an application: sample where the physics is fragile, not where the failure count is high — which is a principled answer to the attractor problem (clip #44 attracts mass because it is hard for reasons the sampler cannot see).

05 SONIC and whole-body control

SONIC ships released ONNX policies and its own MuJoCo harness (gear_sonic/utils/mujoco_sim, decoupled_wbc/sim2mujoco). Newton runs ONNX G1 policies natively. So the concrete integration is:

  1. SONIC-in-Newton conformance. Load the release encoder/decoder in Newton's robot_policy path, match the observation contract (64D token + hands, per the SONIC dataset contract on disk), and confirm it tracks a LAFAN1 clip in SolverMuJoCo as it does in SONIC's own MuJoCo harness. This is the conformance gate the LUCID harness philosophy already asks for.
  2. SONIC fragility map. Run the release policy through the solver set over the LAFAN1 bank. Where does a state-of-the-art WBC controller's behaviour depend on the physics engine? That is a diagnosis of its stability envelope, produced with no training and no hardware — and it is exactly the "analyse deep into stability" the original goal asked for.
  3. Compare against the CLIMB trackers. Same clips, same solvers: does a generalist trained on 100 clips have a different fragility profile than SONIC's 42M-parameter, 100M-frame policy? If SONIC is fragile in the same places, fragility is a property of the motion; if not, it is a property of the training recipe. Both answers are papers.

None of steps 1–3 trains anything. They are evaluation-only, run on frozen assets already on this machine, and each is a few GPU-hours. They can start this week without touching the LUCID priority window.

07 What this means for the plan already in motion

The advisor's v2 queue — E10 (freshness/liveness/cap), E3 (800-clip diversity), E4 (long horizon) — is pre-registered and sound as a sampling study. It is not wrong; it is narrower than the original goal. Two ways to hold both:

  1. Keep the curriculum line as the mjlab-side experiment and let it inherit the physics. Thrust 1's fragility score becomes an L3 prior that is measured rather than read off the reference — which is what A3 said the atlas needed. E10's cap arm and a fragility-weighted arm can share controls.
  2. Run the Newton evaluation-side thrusts in parallel now, since they need no training and no LUCID window. By the time E3 runs post-Sept-15, S1–S4 will have said whether the physics story holds — and if it does, the paper's spine is physics fragility with curriculum as one application, not the reverse.

The decision I am asking for is not "abandon CLIMB". It is whether to re-centre the thesis on Newton's physics capabilities — with the sampler work as supporting evidence — and to fund the five spikes above this week. My recommendation is yes: the strongest findings so far are already about physics, the tooling to test it is on disk, and the evaluation-side work costs no training compute.
