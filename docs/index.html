CLIMB · Robotixx · project page · updated 2026-09-04

Feasibility first: screen the motion, fix the trial, then allocate learning.

CLIMB is an auditable framework for generalist humanoid tracking: a dynamic-feasibility screen, a segment-native paired benchmark, and a grounded curriculum that can adapt only inside verified support. The design follows one finding from a Unitree G1 pipeline: what looks like difficulty is three different things—physical impossibility, missing support, and intrinsic control hardness.

01 · The problem

Humanoid tracking pipelines fail silently, in layers

Motion-tracking foundations (BeyondMimic-style trackers, SONIC-era generalists) share one recipe: retarget a huge mocap corpus, weight training toward failures, evaluate by average survival. Each stage trusts the one before it. Our project began as a curriculum study and became an audit, because every headline effect we chased turned out to be an artifact of a different layer:

Scheduler layer

The failure-adaptive sampler's advertised uniform floor is additive in counts — the real floor is ε/(Σq+ε) < 1 % and shrinks with the number of environments. Collapse is the default, not the exception.

mjlab #1153 · whole_body_tracking #73

Harness layer

Four silent integration errors made the same physics engine — MuJoCo Warp 3.11.0, reached through two integration stacks (mjlab v1.6.0 directly; Newton 7bb6d02d via SolverMuJoCo) — disagree by 40 points of survival: unmirrored domain randomisation, float32 geometry deciding knife-edge contacts, a stale first observation, one-directional state coupling.

plan/S1_RESULT.md · errors #8–11

Data layer

Retargets pass kinematic QC — joint limits, velocities, smoothness — while being dynamically impossible: bodies descending at ~1 g with no contact available. Kinematic checks cannot see missing forces.

plan/N1_RESULT.md · refeas screen

Benchmark layer

Averaging survival over start offsets hides where a policy fails: the attractor clip scored 0.31 because episodes starting after its impossible segment survived. Its true frame-0 survival: 0.00.

reports/N3_baseline_uniform-s1_strat.csv
02 · The investigation

Five acts, each killing the previous explanation

Act 1 — A curriculum loses to uniform sampling

Three seeds of failure-adaptive training under-perform plain uniform (held-out survival 0.780 vs 0.810; per-seed Δ = +0.030/+0.028/+0.030) while pouring 87–89 % of exposure onto a single kneel-and-crawl clip. Deriving the sampler's math exposes the non-floor; both upstream repos notified.

sealed-confirmatory · Exp-1 · reports/campaign_summary_3arm.json

Act 2 — A one-line repair restores coverage

Normalise-then-mix (a true 10 % floor on the simplex) keeps the same failure signal but bounds what it can spend: entropy 0.61 vs 0.39, and it beats the broken sampler by +0.045 endpoint (3/3 seeds) while matching uniform on the sealed primary.

sealed-confirmatory · Exp-2 "Branch B" · plan/BRANCH_DECISION.md

Act 3 — Physics is ruled out, by pre-registration

A paired-counterfactual gate (480+480+480 worlds; delay, motor strength, friction, contact stiffness & model, CoM) fails its own sealed criteria: no intervention changes the attractor's survival in any of ten configurations. Whatever this clip is, it is not parameter-fragile.

sealed negative · G1 gate · reports/G1/run0/

Act 4 — The reference is physically impossible

Contact-free inverse dynamics + a torque-limited contact LP: for a full second of the retargeted descent, no collision geometry is within 6 cm of the floor while the pelvis falls 0.35 m — ~329 N (the robot weighs 327 N) unsupported. The kneel itself is feasible; the bank just never contains it (3.2 % of training duration). The human sat back on their heels; the robot's legs can't fold that far; the retargeter lifted the legs instead of lowering the root.

measurement · N1 · reports/N1_clip44_knee_id.json

Act 5 — Prevalence is corpus-and-pipeline specific

Screening all 10,705 clips (~1 CPU-second each): 22.8 % exceed 10 % infeasible frames — 39 % of ground-contact motions, 59 % of dynamic ones — and the rate spans 0.1 % to 100 % across source datasets under one retargeter. A second production corpus/pipeline measures 0.14 %, so 22.8 % is not a generic retargeted-bank rate. 29 of our own 100 held-out evaluation clips are contaminated.

03 · The claim

Difficulty = feasibility × support × intrinsic

Each factor has its own measurement and its own fix — and a failure-weighted sampler that cannot tell them apart collapses onto the corner where all three meet. This is the paper's spine.

Why this matters beyond one robot: feasibility turned out to be the only difficulty component that transfers across policies (Spearman 0.567 → 0.609 on the headline pair, permutation p = 0.010; direction positive on 6/6 transfer pairs, 4/6 significant beyond a random-feature baseline — the two →grounded pairs move +0.015/+0.018 but do not clear it) — because it is a property of the reference on the robot, not of any training run. Difficulty labels, benchmark rankings, and curricula built without it inherit a policy-specific bias.

06 · The tools

Released instruments, not just claims

refeas — the feasibility screen

Contact-free inverse dynamics + torque-limited contact LP. ~1 CPU-second per clip; MuJoCo + SciPy only; Apache-2.0; G1 worked example (a synthetic hover the screen flags at 45 % infeasible). Screen before training; ship per-clip flags with datasets.

github.com/linjiw/refeas · v0.1.0

Contact-projection repair

The lightweight fix for the recoverable two-thirds: lower the root onto the contact manifold exactly where support is missing (~3 s/clip, joints untouched, over-repair budget refuses genuine ballistics). The attractor: 0.13 → 0.00 infeasible; a 100 %-flagged source subset's walks: 0.66 → 0.01. Strict-set correction: 1,606/2,442 (65.8%) recover through the legacy 15 cm budget.

tools/repair_contact_projection.py · reports/dfrp_v0/census/summary.md · correction C4

Stratified-start evaluation

The sealed fixed-offset harness exposed the "0.31 survival" artifact. A post-outcome audit found unpaired startup randomization, clipped duplicate offsets, and reset-contaminated terminal quality metrics; a paired per-episode v2 is now required.

tools/eval_stratified.py · plan/SEGMENT_NATIVE_FOLLOWUP_2026-08-20.md

Dual-engine conformance protocol

Per-substep paired stepping, an independent third engine as referee, and a shadow solver stepping the other engine's own trajectory. Any second physics implementation coupled to an RL harness deserves this — end-of-episode metrics matched while the harness was broken.

tools/s1_newton_conformance.py

Companion note + upstream notes

4–6 pp audit paper (arXiv/workshop, target Sept 5) carrying the method, prevalence, and recommendations; two upstream drafts (retargeting pipeline; dataset advisory for the 90–100 % sources) awaiting approval before filing.

07 · What's still open

The causal slots — and what would change our minds

Completed interventions are mixed, null, or failed their joint gates. Segment-native v2 now has an exploratory wiring result; confirmatory benefit remains open.

FGAS — soft eligibility sealed ✗

The three-seed soft arm gives Δ −0.0196 on feasible-hard20 and fails its rejected-start-mass gate (0.199). Diagnostics confirm that failure weighting overwhelms a clip-mean soft multiplier; a segment-native follow-up requires a new seal.

N7 — repair the impossible sealed ✗

R/repaired improves over K/raw by +0.0397, but misses the +0.05 SESOI and the coverage rule. R/raw is −0.0036: the gain is reference easing plus co-adaptation, concentrated in over-budget edits, not better raw-reference policy skill.

E3 — support at scale post-Sept 15

800-clip bank with bidirectional named predictions — including 22 clips we claim will get worse. Predicted harms are the sharpest test the support story can face.

Newton predictive gate sealed ✗

Newton v1.5 conformance passed, but the valid 40-unit no-training gate did not: adaptive partial ρ is +0.141 (p = 0.158) and LOCO lift is −0.006. Newton remains an instrument; G3 is killed.

Honest scope

One robot, one reward configuration, survival-centric endpoints, simulation only, 4k-iteration horizon. The solver-ensemble program was explicitly descoped: the second engine earned its keep as a referee and measurement instrument, not an oracle.

08 · Latest research checkpoint

Segment-native training works; survival lift is unresolved exploratory

The GPU lifecycle, bounded curriculum, and paired evaluator pass. One seed shows a motion-quality signal but no established survival benefit. The exact licensed payload now passes 900/900 identities; endpoint-blind calibration is next and confirmatory training has not started.

GPU path and objective repaired

An 8-env lifecycle trace completed 24/24 exact trials with no wrap. Two 512-env arms then ran 200 iterations each (2.46 M steps/arm). A diagnosed early-failure incentive was corrected symmetrically with a failure-only event cost; invalid starts, invalid frames, and censored resets stayed at zero.

reports/segment_v2_smoke/timeline_trace.json · autoresearch/segment-native-260820-2259/research_log.md

Paired survival is inconclusive

On 504 identical worlds per policy, adaptive minus exact-uniform success is +0.0079 (unit-bootstrap 95 % CI −0.0536 to +0.0714); survival is +0.0115 s (−0.0100 to +0.0346). Seeds, starts, initial state, and startup randomization hashes match.

Motion-quality signal

Across 22,321 paired common-survivor frames, adaptive training reduces body-position error by 4.20 mm (95 % CI 1.90–6.63 mm) and anchor-orientation error by 0.0280 rad (0.0163–0.0397). Anchor position, joint error, and work remain unresolved; this is one-seed exploratory evidence.
