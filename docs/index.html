01 · The research question

Failure tells us where learning stops. It does not tell us why.

We want a G1 humanoid to track diverse human motions with a fixed training budget. Repeated failure is useful only if more practice can change the outcome.

A retargeted reference may exceed the model’s contact or actuation assumptions. A valid motion may be poorly represented in the bank. Another may require more practice. These explanations imply different interventions: repair or exclude the reference, improve coverage, or adapt training exposure.

Our current hypothesis is that relative progress over exact admissible support can direct practice toward learnable skills while keeping broad exposure. The four-arm confirmation tests the resulting policy, using the same learner, motion support and training budget.

02 · Findings that changed our approach

Each result narrows what the next experiment must prove.

01
Measured · simulator interface

Admissible support can be enforced throughout a trial.

The current training interface exposes 1,184 admitted motion units and 368,951 legal starts from 800 training motions. A 50-step trial cannot wrap or leave its admitted interval. All 10 completed confirmation runs pass replay with zero recorded invalid-start, invalid-reference or censored-reset events.

How we use this: hold this support fixed while comparing samplers. A separate matched gate-on/off study will test whether exclusion itself improves learning. Model admissibility is not a guarantee of physical robot feasibility.

02
Measured · exploratory fixed-policy simulation

A better physics screen did not translate into an aggregate tracking gain.

The repair study qualified 22 of 26 candidates under its physics screen. Yet the fixed-policy comparison on 26 clips and 656 paired conditions per reference arm gave repaired-minus-raw TrackingScore −0.001003, with clip-bootstrap 95% interval [−0.008588, +0.008020].

How we use this: evaluate repaired references with control outcomes before treating them as better training data. Screen qualification and policy utility answer different questions. This comparison uses one fixed policy; it is not a retraining study.

03
Sealed · earlier E4 decision: not_tested

The original progress rule barely changed exposure.

E4’s absolute-progress arm reached mean allocation TV 0.029659, below its frozen 0.05 gate. Its policy endpoints stayed closed. Replay implicated the fixed additive floor: as absolute progress shrank, allocation approached the prior.

How we use this: normalize progress by its deployment-weighted mean. This preserves sensitivity to relative differences when the whole signal shrinks. E4 did not measure a policy null; the newer confirmation retains the original rule as comparator A.

04
Measured · current confirmation allocation

Relative progress changes exposure in all three confirmation seeds.

R21, R22 and R23 pass their training gates, with mean TV 0.083485, 0.082492 and 0.082333. Conditional-failure sampling D also passes in all three seeds. These results establish that the interventions occurred; their tracking value is still pending.

How we use this: compare R with U to test utility, A to examine the scale correction, and D to ask whether a progress signal offers more than practicing failures. See every completed run and its allocation history.

Sources: verified confirmation export and 5 September exploratory evidence export. TV is total variation from the deployment prior, not TrackingScore.

04 · What would make practice useful?

Separate a changed signal from a recovered skill.

In development seeds 11 and 12, about 46.6% and 46.8% of positive excess allocation mass went to declining success estimates. This is not the share of all samples, and it does not identify forgetting. Three explanations remain open:

Recoverable decline

Hypothesis: a previously learned skill deteriorates and extra practice restores it.

Discriminating test: after independently measuring signal stability, branch matched policies into equal-budget targeted and control practice, then score both on independent paired trials.

Possible use: retain revisiting of declining skills if the extra practice causes recovery. The queued frozen-policy pilot first checks instrumentation; it cannot establish this mechanism.

The next stages each resolve a different uncertainty.

  1. RUNNING
    Confirmation

    Establish whether adaptive exposure improves control.

    Finish the remaining training jobs, run all 48 paired held-out cells, reproduce the frozen analysis and report individual seeds. Keep primary benefit, target-sized benefit ruled out, inconclusive, and invalid/not-tested outcomes distinct.

  2. QUEUED
    Signal pilot

    Build a reliable measure of apparent progress.

    The frozen-policy development pilot waits for confirmation. First verify recording and inference immutability; then design independent repeated scoring and matched practice branches. Use the result to decide whether revisiting declines deserves a future sampler change.

  3. DEVELOPMENT
    H1 · admission

    Measure the learning value of excluding rejected support.

    Compare D with admission on/off over the same 800 clips and candidate partition. CPU training, evaluation and provenance checks pass; full study remains disabled. Finish production analysis/scheduling, fresh-seed audit and GPU smokes, then freeze the separate six-run, 24-cell study. This isolates exclusion within common feasibility-derived boundaries.

  4. DEVELOPMENT
    S1 · physics

    Test which simulator assumptions the result depends on.

    CPU checks cover realized command delay, knee torque caps, foot friction, policy immutability and reset handling. Nine CUDA development cells are queued after confirmation and the signal pilot. A proposed full study evaluates final policies under eight specified conditions, with failures and exposure retained.

  5. PENDING
    Physical evidence

    Connect the findings to an identified robot.

    Prepare 3–5 representative motion families—turning, crouching and dynamic stepping among them—selected from reference kinematics before policy videos. Record all attempts, tracking residuals and interventions. Hardware access, measured actuator/contact fidelity and real G1 transfer remain unconfirmed; simulation sensitivity does not establish Sim2Real.

06 · Working paper story

A contribution to earn, with the current findings visible.

Working abstract · policy results pending

CLIMB: Exact-Support Practice Allocation for Humanoid Motion Tracking

Humanoid imitation learners must decide which motions to practice, but tracking failure can mix reference infeasibility, missing experience and intrinsic control difficulty. We study an exact-support sampling interface that restricts training trials to model-admissible motion intervals and compares four allocation rules under a matched learning budget. A previous absolute-progress rule failed its allocation gate; normalizing the same signal by its deployment-weighted mean sustains exposure contrast in three new confirmation seeds. A separate fixed-policy repair comparison demonstrates no aggregate tracking gain, motivating explicit separation of reference qualification from learning utility. The ongoing four-arm, three-seed study will test final held-out tracking and describe intermediate learning curves. Admission ablation, signal-quality measurement and physical-sensitivity studies are separate evidence requirements. The present results establish interface and intervention validity; improved tracking, recovery from forgetting and hardware transfer remain open.

The intended contribution has three parts: an auditable interface for exact motion support; a matched 12-run policy comparison; and a bounded explanation of admission, signal quality and physical sensitivity. Progress sampling itself is not claimed as a new invention.
