01 · The research question

Failure tells us where learning stops. It does not tell us why.

We want a G1 humanoid to track diverse human motions with a fixed training budget. Repeated failure is useful only if more practice can change the outcome.

A retargeted reference may exceed the model’s contact or actuation assumptions. A valid motion may be poorly represented in the bank. Another may require more practice. These explanations imply different interventions: repair or exclude the reference, improve coverage, or adapt training exposure.

CLIMB separates reference admission from practice allocation. The four-arm confirmation holds the learner, references, support and training budget fixed. It does not establish the proposed relative-progress benefit. A coverage floor cannot create a missing behavior, and apparent progress alone cannot identify intrinsic difficulty or forgetting.

02 · Findings that changed our approach

Each result narrows what the next experiment must prove.

01
Measured · simulator interface

Admissible support can be enforced throughout a trial.

The current training interface exposes 1,184 admitted motion units and 368,951 legal starts from 800 training motions. A 50-step trial cannot wrap or leave its admitted interval. All 12 confirmation runs pass replay with zero recorded invalid-start, invalid-reference or censored-reset events.

How we use this: hold this support fixed while comparing samplers. A separate matched gate-on/off study will test whether exclusion itself improves learning. Model admissibility is not a guarantee of physical robot feasibility.

02
Measured · exploratory fixed-policy simulation

A better physics screen did not translate into an aggregate tracking gain.

The repair study qualified 22 of 26 candidates under its physics screen. Yet the fixed-policy comparison on 26 clips and 656 paired conditions per reference arm gave repaired-minus-raw TrackingScore −0.001003, with clip-bootstrap 95% interval [−0.008588, +0.008020].

How we use this: evaluate repaired references with control outcomes before treating them as better training data. Screen qualification and policy utility answer different questions. This comparison uses one fixed policy; it is not a retraining study.

03
Sealed · earlier E4 decision: not_tested

The original progress rule barely changed exposure.

E4’s absolute-progress arm reached mean allocation TV 0.029659, below its frozen 0.05 gate. Its policy endpoints stayed closed. Replay implicated the fixed additive floor: as absolute progress shrank, allocation approached the prior.

How we use this: normalize progress by its deployment-weighted mean. This preserves sensitivity to relative differences when the whole signal shrinks. E4 did not measure a policy null; the newer confirmation retains the original rule as comparator A.

04
Measured · current confirmation allocation

Relative progress changes exposure in all three confirmation seeds.

R21, R22 and R23 pass their training gates, with mean TV 0.083485, 0.082492 and 0.082333. Conditional-failure sampling D also passes in all three seeds. These results establish that the interventions occurred. The completed held-out comparison does not establish the registered tracking benefit.

How we use this: keep the measured allocation contrast separate from the inconclusive policy result. Compare R with U, A and D under the stated limits; R−D is not a pure ranking-only intervention. See every completed run and its allocation history.

Sources: verified confirmation export and 5 September exploratory evidence export. TV is total variation from the deployment prior, not TrackingScore.

03 · The completed controlled experiment

Same support. Same learner. Four rules for practice.

Unitree G1 in pinned mjlab 1.6.0; 512 environments; 4,000 PPO iterations per run; paired training seeds 21, 22 and 23. All training gates passed before the 48 held-out cells opened. The complete campaign uses 49,152,000 simulator transitions per policy.

Relative progress before active caps: p(u) = 0.8 b(u) + 0.2 b(u) g(u) / E_b[g], for complete history and positive mean progress. The fallback and caps remain part of the implemented rule. Common positive rescaling of progress leaves this mixture unchanged, and pre-cap total variation is at most 0.2. These are allocation properties, not evidence that the selected practice is valuable.

Exact temporal support: every reference frame accessed over the trial, including configured observation lookahead, must remain in the admitted interval. Exactness describes support membership under the declared model and screen; it does not certify closed-loop or physical feasibility.

The primary test: final-checkpoint R−U feasible-hard TrackingScore gain ≥ +0.02, a positive lower two-sided 95% paired seed-level t bound, and an all-panel lower bound above −0.01. Evaluation covers 100 held-out clips, including 25 feasible-hard clips. Three trained seeds determine replication; thousands of conditions do not create more independent policies.

Checkpoints 1000, 2000, 3000 and 3999 also reveal learning curves. Earlier attainment of U’s final score is an exploratory efficiency measure with explicit non-attainment, not a result we assume in advance. A supplementary paired hierarchical bootstrap cannot override the frozen primary decision.

04 · What would make practice useful?

Separate a changed signal from a recovered skill.

In development seeds 11 and 12, about 46.6% and 46.8% of positive excess allocation mass went to declining success estimates. This is not the share of all samples, and it does not identify forgetting. Three explanations remain open:

Recoverable decline

Hypothesis: a previously learned skill deteriorates and extra practice restores it.

Discriminating test: after independently measuring signal stability, branch matched policies into equal-budget targeted and control practice, then score both on independent paired trials.

Possible use: retain revisiting of declining skills if the extra practice causes recovery. The completed frozen-policy pilot checks instrumentation; it does not establish this mechanism or the estimator’s noise floor.

    Confirmation

    Preserve the inconclusive policy result.

    All 12 runs and 48 cells are complete and independently reproduced. No registered benefit or efficiency-accelerator claim is supported. Do not append seeds or change thresholds to obtain a favorable decision.

  2. NEXT
    C1 · continuous

    Test usable execution beyond short windows.

    The existing held-out test already uses uninterrupted three-second windows. A new CPU development adapter completes four attempts on two entire admitted training references lasting 8.58 and 6.12 seconds, with one fixed development policy and no intermediate resets. This is pipeline evidence only. Validate failure retirement and CUDA execution, fix reference-only selection, then compare U/D/R on the same continuous sequences. Never join disconnected admitted intervals across rejected transitions.

  3. NEXT
    H1 · admission

    Measure whether excluding rejected practice helps learning.

    Compare D with admission on/off over the same 800 clips and candidate partition, disclosing normalization. CPU training, evaluation and provenance checks pass; full study remains disabled pending the remaining production checks and separate freeze. This tests admission under D, not an admission-by-relative-progress interaction.

  4. PILOT COMPLETE
    Signal value

    Distinguish recoverable skill from apparent progress.

    Instrumentation passes; stationarity and the noise floor remain unestablished. Use independent repeated scoring at training-relevant sample counts. A proposed three-development-seed, two-checkpoint, targeted/control design gives twelve equal-budget practice branches. Freeze selection and analysis before their outcomes. Reliability-calibrated progress is a candidate only if diagnosis warrants a change.

  5. DEVELOPMENT
    Physical sensitivity

    Validate the runtime before interpreting perturbation results.

    CPU lifecycle checks cover delay, torque caps, friction and policy immutability. After correcting an assertion about an optional camera/raycast graph, all nine CUDA development cells completed. The aggregate original-versus-unchanged exact CSV parity check failed, so full sensitivity evaluation remains disabled. Numerical parity diagnosis is pending. No robustness benefit follows from development checks.

  6. UNCONFIRMED
    Hardware

    Test the same allocation claim on a common robot interface.

    First audit actor observations, export and actions. Proposed nominal references: turning, crouching and dynamic stepping, two per family, selected before comparing policies. Use U/D/R with common adaptation and initial conditions; retain every attempt, intervention and failure. Hardware access and CLIMB transfer are unconfirmed.

06 · Working paper story

A contribution to earn, with the current findings visible.

Working abstract · complete simulation result

CLIMB: Exact-Support Curriculum Learning for Humanoid Motion Tracking

A failed tracking attempt does not establish that additional practice is valuable. CLIMB separates model-based reference admission from learner-dependent allocation, binding each training trial to exact temporal support under a declared screen. We compare uniform, absolute-progress, relative-progress and conditional-failure allocation with unchanged references, a common learner and matched training budgets. Across three training seeds, relative progress changes exposure but does not establish the registered held-out tracking benefit: final feasible-hard R−U TrackingScore is −0.01507, with two-sided seed-level 95% interval [−0.09436, +0.06422]. The all-panel non-regression criterion also does not pass. Exploratory learning curves do not support an efficiency-accelerator claim. These findings motivate separate tests of admission value, signal reliability and the causal value of targeted practice. Continuous-execution development verifies longer reference traversal on two training motions; comparative continuous execution and hardware transfer remain pending.

The intended contribution has three parts: an auditable interface for exact motion support; a matched 12-run policy comparison; and a bounded explanation of admission, signal quality and physical sensitivity. Progress sampling itself is not claimed as a new invention. ALP-GMM provides learning-progress precedent; LIMMT studies motion curation, while GMT and EGM include adaptive motion sampling. CLIMB’s distinction to test is exact temporal support paired with controlled evidence about the value of practice.
